## Target Users

### Patients (Consumer Side)

**Primary:** Adults with chronic conditions who have accumulated medical records fragmented across multiple providers.

- **2.4 billion** people globally live with at least one chronic condition (WHO, 2022)
- Each has medical records distributed across hospitals, labs, pharmacies, and imaging centers
- Average patient in India sees 3–4 different providers per year; records are never consolidated
- These patients currently have no way to query, share, or monetize their own medical history

**Initial beachhead:** South Asian patients (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka combined ~1.8B population). GenomeChain is built at ETHGlobal Mumbai. The founding team has direct access to this user base. World ID has significant Orb deployment across India. This population is severely underrepresented in GWAS databases — making their data disproportionately valuable for research.

**Secondary consumer segment:** Patients with rare diseases actively searching for trials. This segment is highly motivated to participate — they seek trials, not the other way around. ProtocolRegistry's pre-registered study index gives them a discoverable, trustable list.

### Research Institutions (Institutional Side)

**Pharma companies:** Need diverse, consented patient cohorts for GWAS, pharmacogenomics, and RWE studies. Currently pay $250K–$5M per study to data brokers who buy insurance claims data. Patients receive $0 from this market. GenomeChain routes payment directly to patients, reducing total cost while improving data quality (genome-anchored vs. insurance codes) and consent provenance.

**Academic research institutions:** Need patient cohorts for academic studies. Currently constrained by IRB-approved institutional patient pools, which skew toward patients of the institution's local population. Arkiv's global patient base breaks this constraint.

**CROs (Contract Research Organizations):** Run trials on behalf of pharma. Currently spend significant budget on patient recruitment (average $6,500 per recruited patient in Phase III). GenomeChain's pre-consented, genomically-matched cohort reduces recruitment cost and time.

## Post-Hackathon Pilot Plan

### Phase 1 — Hospital Partnership (Q1–Q2 2027)

Three-way pilot with Indian hospital networks:

**Apollo Hospitals (pan-India):** 71 hospitals, 10,000+ beds, 11M+ patients annually. Apollo has an existing digital health platform (Apollo 24|7). Integration target: patient record export from Apollo 24|7 → GenomeChain upload widget. Patient earns GNM for uploading their own Apollo records.

**Fortis Healthcare:** 36 hospitals. Existing patient portal. Same integration model.

**AIIMS (All India Institute of Medical Sciences):** Government research hospital. Primary interest is on the research side — AIIMS runs GWAS studies and needs diverse Indian genomic cohorts. Arkiv integration gives them a consented, queryable patient database without the 18-month data-sharing negotiation process.

### Phase 2 — Genomic Lab Partnership (Q3 2027)

**Strand Life Sciences (Bangalore):** India's largest genomics lab. Integration target: patient receives genome report from Strand → one-click upload to GenomeChain. Strand already has the VCF file; we provide the LabAttestation infrastructure.

**MedGenome:** Clinical genomics lab focused on rare diseases and hereditary cancers. Same integration model. High-motivation patient segment.

### Phase 3 — Research Marketplace Launch (Q4 2027)

With 50,000+ patients onboarded from hospital pilots, launch the institutional marketplace:

- 5 pharma companies with active India studies (Cipla, Sun Pharma, Dr. Reddy's, Lupin, Zydus)
- Target: 3 live studies with active patient consent and Arkiv queries by end of 2027
- Measure: cost per recruited patient vs. traditional CRO recruitment

## Why This Reaches People Who Weren't Reached Before

The existing genomic marketplace landscape (23andMe, AncestryDNA, Nebula Genomics) reached educated, affluent, primarily North American and European users willing to pay $99–$299 for a consumer genetics kit. The research value of this cohort is high but its diversity is low.

GenomeChain's model is inverted: **patients earn money for their data rather than paying for a service.** The 500 GNM genome upload reward and 10 GNM per medical record creates a direct financial incentive for participation, even among users with no prior interest in genetics. The World App (Orb-verified, 160+ countries) provides the distribution channel to populations that have never been part of a genomic study.

This is the structural change: from "who can afford to pay for a consumer genetics kit" to "who has medical records and wants to earn from them." These are fundamentally different and much larger populations.
